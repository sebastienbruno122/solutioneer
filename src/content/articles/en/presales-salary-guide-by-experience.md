Presales compensation varies more than most generic career pages suggest — by experience, industry, and company stage far more than by job title alone. Here's a realistic breakdown, with the factors that actually explain the spread.

*Methodology note: the ranges below are directional estimates based on observed hiring patterns in the B2B SaaS/tech market (published job postings, field reporting), not a formal compensation survey. Treat them as a starting reference point, and cross-check against data specific to your market and industry before negotiating.*

## How presales compensation is structured

Before the numbers themselves, one structural point matters: presales compensation in most companies combines a **base salary** and a **variable component** (commonly called OTE — On Target Earnings), with the variable typically representing 10-20% of total target pay — much lower than a pure sales role's 30-50%+. That variable is usually tied to overall team or segment attainment rather than a strict individual quota.

A number quoted without specifying whether it's base-only or full OTE isn't directly comparable across postings — worth clarifying explicitly before comparing offers.

## Indicative ranges by experience level

| Level | Experience | Indicative base (annual) | Indicative OTE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junior | 0-2 years | $70,000 – $95,000 | $80,000 – $110,000 |
| Mid-level | 3-5 years | $95,000 – $130,000 | $110,000 – $150,000 |
| Senior | 6-9 years | $125,000 – $165,000 | $145,000 – $190,000 |
| Principal / Architect | 10+ years | $155,000 – $200,000+ | $180,000 – $230,000+ |

These figures reflect roles at mid-to-large B2B SaaS companies in major US tech hubs. Regional and industry factors below can shift these numbers meaningfully in either direction.

## What actually drives the range

### Industry

High-complexity, long-cycle industries — cybersecurity, cloud infrastructure, enterprise data/AI platforms — tend to pay 10-20% above the averages above, reflecting how scarce it is to find people who can hold their own technically against very sharp buyers.

### Company stage and size

An early-stage, high-growth startup may offer a below-average base, offset by meaningful equity that doesn't show up in a base/OTE table. Established enterprises and public companies tend to offer higher base salaries with proportionally smaller variable components.

### Geography and remote policy

Fully remote roles increasingly compress geographic pay gaps compared to a few years ago, though companies with location-based pay bands still adjust meaningfully for cost of living.

### Where the company sits internationally

Multinational companies hiring into a specific country's presales team sometimes benchmark against global pay standards rather than purely local market rates, particularly to retain senior talent against international competition for the same skill set.

## How to use these numbers

Three practical steps before negotiating or evaluating an offer:

1. **Always separate base from full OTE** in an offer, and ask explicitly if it isn't broken out.
2. **Compare within the same industry and company stage**, not just job title — a cybersecurity sales engineer and a vertical SaaS solutions consultant aren't directly comparable despite similar titles.
3. **Factor in equity, remote flexibility, and learning budget** as part of total comparison, especially against early-stage offers with lower cash compensation.
